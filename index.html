Leadership: strategy for a local commune

As team leader of 14 students over 15 weeks, I led a field-based study of the economic development of Tề Lỗ commune (Phú Thọ Province, formerly Vĩnh Phúc), a hub of metal and vehicle recycling. We worked with local leaders on site, interviewed 10 local business households, cross-checked what we heard against documents with our lecturer's guidance, and turned it into a strategy orientation report for 2026–2030, submitted back to the local authority.

14team members led15weeks, from fieldwork to report10business households interviewed

What the evidence showed

  • Value of production around VND 6,268bn in 2025, but growth rests on manual, low value-added work.
  • 590 household businesses and 120 enterprises barely use ERP, CRM or data systems, despite near-full 4G and fibre coverage.
  • 1,600+ land-use violations and a wastewater station left idle are the binding constraints.

What we proposed

  • Vision: a specialised industrial town and green recycling hub for the south of the province.
  • Three phases: clear land and restore the environment (2026–28), complete the industrial cluster and circular economy (2028–30), then EV and lithium battery recycling.
  • A priority project list with KPIs and a funding plan led by private and household capital.

Project: competitor landscape

A work sample from an internship task: comparing how competing photography services price and package their offers for international visitors in two Vietnamese cities, and which services customers mention in public reviews.

Confidentiality: company, client and competitor details are masked or partially hidden to protect data. Figures are rounded and shown for illustration.

What the data shows: some competitors charge a flat fee whatever the group size, others scale per person (up to roughly 4x for four guests). Pose guidance and costume rental come up most in reviews, so customers value help on the day, not only the photos.
